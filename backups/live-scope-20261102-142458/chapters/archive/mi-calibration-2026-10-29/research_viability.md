# Research viability: Chapter 1

**Verdict: PROVISIONAL.** The broad claim “accurate MI command interpretation with substantially reduced calibration” is not an original gap as written. The supporting contribution could be a narrow, reproducible comparison of calibration burden against decision reliability, but its distinction from Rao et al. (2026) remains uncertain until their full protocol is compared. The required live prototype is not feasible within the stated US$300 ceiling using the verified equipment examined here.

## Originality

Three closest competitors already establish the main ingredients:

1. **Rao et al. (2026), “Calibration-Free Online Detection in Wearable Motor Imagery Brain-Computer Interfaces,” [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TNSRE.2026.3695774).** The abstract reports a few-channel wearable MI headband, a subject-independent model trained on data from 100 healthy participants, and supervised self-training from labelled online data. Accuracy reportedly rose from 69% to 81% and 86% after two updates, with 25 healthy participants in online experiments and 10 stroke participants. The full text available in the search record describes four wet EEG sites (C3, C4, O1, O2), a forehead electrode, and 250 Hz sampling. This directly defeats “first calibration-free MI BCI,” “no user data required,” and “few-channel calibration-free MI is unexplored.” It is also very close to the proposed low-channel live demonstrator. “Calibration-free” here means no **offline** calibration; labelled online updates still use user-specific information. A protocol-level comparison is still needed for the amount and timing of labels, thresholding/rejection criteria, and how “calibration time” was counted.

2. **Xiong and Wei (2022), “Reducing calibration time in motor imagery-based BCIs by data alignment and empirical mode decomposition,” [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0263641).** They use Euclidean alignment and generated training examples with LDA/logistic regression, comparing against real-only and unaligned augmentation on two MI datasets. Thus limited-label MI calibration, alignment, augmentation and classical classifiers are established. The abstract supports reduced training-data needs for the reported datasets; it does not establish live online control or false activations during unconstrained inactivity.

3. **Song et al. (2022), “An EEG-based asynchronous MI-BCI system to reduce false positives with a small number of channels for neurorehabilitation,” [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnbot.2022.971547).** This pilot used participant-specific calibration and threshold adjustment with healthy and stroke participants. It directly establishes that false-positive rejection in asynchronous MI has been studied. Its reported sensitivity was below 30%, illustrating the cost of rejection; its rehabilitation task, selected channels and protocol differ from the proposed study.

Ma et al. (2022) also document cross-session MI variability and adaptation ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-022-01647-1)); cross-session drift cannot be presented as a novel problem.

**Unsupported novelty claims:** first calibration-free or calibration-free MI system; first use of few-channel EEG for that purpose; first use of supervised online updates, alignment, limited-label adaptation, or thresholds to reject unwanted commands; novelty based on first implementation in Zimbabwe. A combination of these established pieces is not automatically novel.

**Narrow question that remains testable, with novelty unresolved:** *On public MI datasets with held-out participants and explicitly labelled rest, how does a fixed calibration budget (zero, 5, 10 or 20 labelled trials per class) change the trade-off between accepted-command accuracy and false command events per minute for a pooled CSP–LDA decoder, compared with user-specific CSP–LDA?* Use participant-level held-out testing, choose thresholds on separate validation data, and count every target label used for tuning as calibration. PhysioNet’s 109-person dataset has 64-channel 160 Hz EEG and cued task/rest runs ([dataset record](https://physionet.org/content/eegmmidb/1.0.0/)); its constrained rest is only a proxy for inactivity. Report false events only over labelled non-control exposure and state its duration. This is a defensible **comparative engineering evaluation**, not a new algorithm or demonstrated literature gap. It may be subsumed by Rao et al.; do not claim originality until their full text and the exact data split/outcomes are checked against this question.

## Physical prototype feasibility

**Verdict: not feasible within US$300 on verified prices; no safe, complete compatible purchase within budget was established.** For MI, acquisition must provide raw, time-resolved EEG from suitable motor-region locations, particularly around C3/C4; the proposed chapter’s three-channel C3/Cz/C4 montage also needs stable reference and ground arrangements. Rao’s wearable work itself used C3/C4 among its four scalp sites. A low-cost board alone does not provide electrodes, suitable placement hardware, conductive medium where required, acquisition interface, or delivered cost.

OpenBCI’s four-channel Ganglion is listed at **US$624.99**. It samples at 200 Hz and its documentation describes access to raw channel data ([board](https://shop.openbci.com/products/ganglion-board), [sampling/raw format](https://docs.openbci.com/Ganglion/GanglionDataFormat/)). The vendor says electrodes are separate and a Ganglion dongle is required; its board page lists the dongle separately and states customs/import fees are excluded ([requirements and import notice](https://shop.openbci.com/products/ganglion-board)). A 10-electrode gold-cup cable is **US$44.99**, but requires conductive paste ([electrodes](https://shop.openbci.com/products/openbci-gold-cup-electrodes)). The resulting board + dongle (US$19.99) + cups subtotal is **US$689.97**, before paste, any placement/adapter needs, shipping, import charges and contingency. A more complete Ganglion starter bundle is listed at **US$1,019.99** and still excludes shipping/import; the vendor says it supports real-time raw EEG, but suggests adding gold cups and paste for wet-electrode EEG ([bundle contents and price](https://shop.openbci.com/products/biosensing-starter-bundle)). These are vendor listings, not an independent safety certification or Zimbabwe delivery quote. No human recording should use this or another device until the institution and supervisor approve its electrical safety and recording protocol.

### Paradigm decision

- **MI:** Retain provisionally for the offline research question. Its motor-cortex channel requirements fit the existing datasets and the research question; a four-channel device can be technically relevant only if it can be fitted at the required sites with raw streaming and safe, approved use. The verified complete-equipment cost fails the budget.
- **P300:** Reconsider only if a justified visual or auditory communication task is preferred. It uses event-locked responses, often including midline/parietal sites such as Pz, and needs timed stimuli/markers and enough labelled trials. Fewer channels may be workable in some systems, but no affordable, suitable acquisition setup is verified here; reduced channels do not solve the amplifier cost.
- **SSVEP:** Reconsider only if visual selection suits the intended user and flicker is acceptable. It generally requires occipital sites (e.g. Oz/O1/O2), a stimulus display with reliable timing, and raw EEG. EOG/gaze hybrids already exist; they add equipment and accessibility/privacy questions. No cheaper complete setup is established.

**Recommendation:** keep MI as the conditional paradigm because it best matches the existing calibration question and public-data plan, but do not promise the physical prototype. P300 or SSVEP does not currently resolve the acquisition-budget blocker. Obtain a complete delivered quotation or confirmed institutional equipment access before freezing the paradigm; verify the target electrode locations, raw stream, sampling, drivers, reference/ground arrangement and safety documentation for that exact setup.

## Three decisions before finalizing Chapter 1

1. **Original contribution:** after checking Rao et al.’s full protocol against the narrowed question, approve the comparative evaluation as the contribution or change/narrow the research question. No algorithmic novelty is presently supported.
2. **Equipment and budget:** identify an institutionally approved device or equipment loan with a complete Zimbabwe-delivered cost at or below US$300, or obtain formal agreement to change the budget/prototype requirement. A board-only price is insufficient.
3. **Paradigm and application:** confirm MI versus a specific P300/SSVEP task only after equipment channel placement and participant accessibility are checked; confirm the supervisor/ethics route before any live human recording.

## Evidence limits

This assessment uses the existing Chapter 1 matrix and gap analysis, plus targeted checks of Rao’s indexed abstract/full-text record, dataset records, and manufacturer documentation and product listings. The Rao details and results above are author-reported; full independent protocol comparison and re-analysis were not performed. No complete shipping/import quotation to Zimbabwe, locally available certified device, institutional equipment access, or approved safety status was verified. Price and availability can change. Conclusions about no affordable alternative are therefore limited to the verified products, not every possible local or second-hand source.
