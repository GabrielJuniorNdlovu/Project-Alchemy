## 1.1 Introduction

A brain–computer interface (BCI) interprets measured brain activity to support communication or control. In non-invasive electroencephalography (EEG), electrodes placed on the scalp record electrical potential differences associated with brain activity. A motor-imagery (MI) BCI uses patterns associated with imagined movements to estimate an intended command. Such systems offer a possible way to interact with computers and external devices, although distinguishing motor-imagery signals and interpreting them across recording sessions remain challenging (Tangermann et al., 2012).
An MI decoder may produce an incorrect prediction when familiar imagery classes are difficult to distinguish or recording conditions differ from those encountered during training. It may also assign a familiar label to an unfamiliar imagery task. Changes in the signals from individual EEG channels can introduce further uncertainty. If an incorrect prediction is accepted as a command, it may cause an unintended action; withholding too many predictions, however, can reduce the user's opportunities to control the system.
Risk-aware selective decoding addresses this trade-off by allowing the system to accept or withhold a predicted command according to the available evidence. This project investigates that decision under three conditions: changes between recording sessions, motor-imagery classes withheld during development, and simulated channel degradation. Its evaluation will consider both the errors among accepted commands and the rate at which commands are accepted.
The proposed engineering product is an eight-channel MI BCI intended to support robotic-arm control and computer interaction. The project includes reproducible experimental evaluation and a Raspberry Pi 4 demonstrator using prerecorded EEG replay. A physical headset and approved live acquisition are conditional engineering deliverables; replay alone does not demonstrate live operation. This chapter presents the background, problem, aim and objectives, followed by the scope, feasibility, significance and work plan.
