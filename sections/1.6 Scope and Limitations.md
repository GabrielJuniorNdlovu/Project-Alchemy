## 1.6 Scope and Limitations of the Project

The project covers a wearable eight-channel EEG acquisition system, personal motor-imagery data collection, subject-specific model development, selective command acceptance and Raspberry Pi 4 deployment. The intended final demonstration uses incoming EEG to control predefined robotic-arm and computer navigation or selection actions. Recorded-data replay and simulated interfaces are intermediate development tools.

The experimental dataset will contain only the researcher's own recordings across multiple occasions. Familiar imagery will initially concern the left and right hand. Additional imagery tasks may be collected for an unfamiliar-class experiment, subject to protocol feasibility. Those tasks will be excluded from decoder and reliability-gate fitting and calibration for that experiment. Defined channel perturbations will be applied to copies of held-out recordings; the study will not equate them with verified physical electrode failures.

Model selection, preprocessing and gate fitting will use permitted development data. Calibration access will be documented separately from final testing. The investigation will compare established models and rejection approaches under a reproducible protocol; it does not claim a new classifier architecture or guaranteed performance gain. Control will initially use explicit command opportunities and an application state machine. Unrestricted asynchronous control, clinical treatment and population-level assistive effectiveness are outside the demonstrated scope.

One participant limits generalization to other users, and repeated sessions may remain correlated. A small number of independent sessions could make estimates imprecise even when many windows are available. Task labels indicate instructed imagery rather than verified mental execution. Recording artifacts, fatigue, electrode replacement, learning and feedback may affect results and must be documented. Offline prediction performance cannot establish live task performance.

Human recording requires institutional ethics authorization and electrical-safety assessment of the complete connected system. Consent, stopping procedures, skin comfort, fatigue, secure storage and retention must be covered even for self-recording. The researcher will retain raw data and acquisition logs with controlled access and document any exclusions.

Equipment, delivered cost and approval dates are unconfirmed. The live system remains the intended deliverable, but completion cannot be promised under the current resources. If personal acquisition is blocked before data exist, empirical model evaluation is also blocked. Any partial outcome will distinguish software or bench work, offline personal-data results, embedded replay and live interaction actually completed.
