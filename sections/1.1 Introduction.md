## 1.1 Introduction

This chapter introduces a proposed wearable eight-channel electroencephalography (EEG)-based motor-imagery brain–computer interface for live robotic-arm control and computer interaction. The project combines the development of an acquisition and embedded control system with an investigation of whether predicted commands should be accepted or withheld. Its research focus is the balance between incorrect accepted commands and the availability of useful commands when recording conditions change.

The study will use the researcher's own labelled EEG recordings collected across multiple sessions, subject to institutional ethics authorization and assessment of the complete acquisition system's electrical safety. The intended system will process incoming EEG on a Raspberry Pi 4 and translate accepted predictions into predefined actions. Recorded-data replay will support development before live integration. The chapter presents the background and context, problem statement, aim and objectives, scope and limitations, feasibility, significance and work plan. It distinguishes the intended live system from intermediate experiments and identifies the resources and approvals needed to complete it.
