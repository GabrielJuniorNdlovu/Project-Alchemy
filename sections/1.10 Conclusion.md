## 1.10 Conclusion

This chapter establishes the proposed wearable eight-channel motor-imagery BCI for live robotic-arm and computer interaction. The engineering work encompasses personal EEG acquisition, embedded processing and application control. The scientific investigation concerns whether selective command acceptance can reduce incorrect accepted commands while preserving availability under recording-session variation, unfamiliar imagery and simulated channel degradation.

The study will use the researcher's own recordings across multiple sessions and compare established decoding and rejection approaches through a reproducible protocol. Its findings will be limited to the participant and conditions observed. Replay will support development and validation before live integration; it will not establish live reliability.

Completion depends on an approved acquisition system, institutional ethics authorization, adequate personal data and physical integration. The planned setup fits within the researcher's US$200 cost assessment; the schedule and technical performance still require validation. Subsequent work will justify the acquisition and evaluation protocol, address those dependencies and report both successful and incomplete outcomes accurately.
