## 1.7 Feasibility Study

### Technical feasibility

The proposed system will integrate an eight-channel PiEEG-8 acquisition front end, a wearable electrode arrangement and Raspberry Pi 4 processing with robotic-arm and computer-interaction interfaces. Development will proceed through acquisition checks, model evaluation, recorded-data replay and live integration. Signal quality, timing, driver operation and component compatibility will be assessed during implementation before reliable operation is claimed.

Institutional ethics authorization and an electrical-safety assessment of the complete connected system are required before human EEG recording. The acquisition board, power arrangement and connections to external equipment must be assessed together.

### Economic feasibility

Based on the researcher's procurement assessment, the PiEEG-8 is available for US$100 and the complete planned setup fits within a total budget of US$200. This includes all remaining equipment, accessories, delivery and import costs. The project is therefore financially feasible on the supplied costing.

| Budget component | Planned cost or limit |
|---|---:|
| PiEEG-8 acquisition board | US$100 |
| Remaining equipment, accessories, delivery and import costs | Within the remaining US$100 |
| Total project budget ceiling | US$200 |

These figures represent the researcher's cost assessment rather than independently verified supplier quotations. Individual prices for the remaining items have not been specified, so no itemised allocations are assumed. Procurement records will document actual expenditure against the budget. The project is intended to produce an academic research prototype; no direct financial return is assumed.

### Social feasibility

The research will use the researcher's own EEG recordings, avoiding dependence on recruiting additional participants. The recording procedure will account for comfort, fatigue, electrode hygiene and the ability to stop a session. Institutional ethics requirements, appropriate consent documentation and secure storage of personal recordings remain applicable. Findings will be limited to the participant and conditions studied.

### Operational feasibility

The planned workflow covers equipment integration, repeated-session recording, decoder and reliability-gate development, Raspberry Pi deployment and live application testing. A phased approach allows faults to be identified before advancing to the next stage. Predefined interaction commands and a documented setup procedure will support repeatable operation.

The proposed 16-week schedule will be assessed against equipment delivery, approval lead times, recording requirements and integration progress. Completion within that period remains a planning objective requiring validation. The final evaluation will document the live system's command behaviour, processing delays and task performance, together with any incomplete work.
