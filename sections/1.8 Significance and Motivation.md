## 1.8 Significance and Motivation for the Project

Access to computers supports communication, education, work and participation in everyday life. People with severe motor impairments may have difficulty using a conventional keyboard or mouse. This project is motivated by the possibility that motor-imagery brain–computer interfaces could provide an additional way to select computer commands when direct movement is limited.

The proposed system will investigate this possibility through a wearable eight-channel EEG interface intended to support predefined computer navigation and selection actions, alongside robotic-arm control. Its selective-decoding function will assess whether a predicted command should be accepted or withheld. This matters for assistive interaction because an incorrect accepted command may cause an unintended action, while excessive rejection may make the interface less useful. The project will examine this balance as an engineering question rather than assume that the system will provide reliable or independent access.

The humanitarian relevance lies in exploring a potential alternative input pathway for people who cannot readily use standard physical controls. Recent work has demonstrated non-invasive EEG-based robotic hand control in experienced users (Ding et al., 2025), while a randomized pilot study of MI-BCI rehabilitation for people with severe post-stroke upper-limb paresis found no significant difference from conventional rehabilitation on its primary functional outcome (Brunner et al., 2024). These studies illustrate both the assistive potential being investigated and the need to avoid assuming benefit. A working prototype could inform future research into accessible computer interaction and assistive technologies. However, the planned recordings are from one participant, and the project is not a clinical trial or an evaluation with people with disabilities. Its findings will not establish usability, safety, accessibility or benefit for that population. Further co-design, ethical review and evaluation with intended users would be needed before making those claims.

The project's contribution will be a documented subject-specific investigation of command reliability and availability across recording sessions, together with the implementation and measured behaviour of the embedded system. Results may show practical value, limited benefit or unresolved technical challenges. Each outcome can guide future engineering work, provided conclusions remain within the evidence collected.
