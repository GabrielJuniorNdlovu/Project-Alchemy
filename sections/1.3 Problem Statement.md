## 1.3 Problem Statement

The proposed wearable eight-channel motor-imagery brain–computer interface must convert newly acquired EEG into reliable commands for a robotic arm and computer interface. A familiar-class prediction can be incorrect, and accepting it may produce an unintended action. Withholding predictions can prevent some actions, but excessive rejection reduces opportunities for control. The problem is therefore to determine whether command acceptance can be made more reliable while retaining useful availability in a live system developed from the researcher's own EEG.

Three conditions will be investigated separately. Recording-session variability concerns differences between earlier development recordings and later sessions; Ma et al. (2022) examined this issue through distinct session-based evaluations. Unfamiliar imagery concerns tasks outside the decoder's command vocabulary that may nevertheless receive a familiar label. Simulated channel degradation concerns defined disturbances applied to recorded signals. Recognizing errors among familiar classes does not establish detection of unfamiliar tasks or robustness to corrupted channels. These experiments must preserve independent test sessions and avoid using unfamiliar test classes to tune the detector.

Existing research has already addressed false-positive rejection in a small-channel MI-BCI (Song et al., 2022). Other MI work has examined uncertainty estimation for identifying potentially incorrect predictions, including a cross-subject comparison in which conventional softmax outputs were competitive with some advanced uncertainty methods (Manivannan et al., 2024). Rejection and reliability estimation are therefore established approaches, and their inclusion is not itself a novel contribution. This project will investigate whether a lightweight mechanism using classification confidence, feature novelty and signal-quality indicators offers a useful comparison with established rejection methods under the specified personal recording conditions. The available evidence does not establish that this combination is superior or that the exact comparison constitutes an unaddressed literature gap.

The primary question is: for this participant, can risk-aware selective decoding reduce errors among accepted familiar-class commands at comparable acceptance rates to established rejection methods, while limiting acceptance of unfamiliar imagery under session variability and simulated channel degradation? Errors on familiar tasks and acceptance of unfamiliar tasks will be reported separately. Because the recordings come from one person, repeated trials cannot establish cross-subject generalization or population-level robustness; the contribution must be evaluated as a subject-specific longitudinal and engineering investigation.

The intended outcome also requires live acquisition, Raspberry Pi 4 processing and application control, with measured delays and observed command behaviour. Replay is an intermediate validation stage. Human-connected recording requires institutional ethics authorization and complete-system electrical-safety assessment. If acquisition cannot proceed and no authorized personal recordings exist, the empirical investigation is blocked as well as the live demonstration. Command gating will complement explicit application-state control and independent robotic stopping mechanisms; it will not establish physical safety on its own.
